# Subject-appropriate learning design

Route activities by the evidence the subject requires.

- **English Language**: close/extensive reading, purposeful vocabulary and grammar in context, oral interaction, process writing, text comparison, multimodal composition, conferencing, criteria-based peer/self assessment, and revision for a real audience.
- **Mathematics**: explicit concept development, carefully varied examples, multiple representations, worked-example study, deliberate practice, reasoning, problem solving, retrieval, error analysis, mathematical communication, and transfer. Do not force projects, AI, or presentations.
- **Science**: questioning, modelling, fair testing, practical investigation, measurement uncertainty, data quality, safe technique, evidence explanation, and claim evaluation.
- **Humanities**: source inquiry, provenance/reliability, case comparison, spatial/temporal reasoning, fieldwork, interpretation, and evidence-based argument.
- **Technology**: needs analysis, design criteria, systems/computational thinking, making, testing, debugging, iteration, safety, privacy, and justified trade-offs.
- **Arts**: experiencing/appreciating, research, technique, experimentation, making/performing, critique, rehearsal, portfolio/process documentation, and revision; distinguish individual from group evidence. For sourced, digital, collage, or AI-assisted work, record source, licence/permission, attribution, access date, permitted transformation, and the boundary of the student's own authorship in Resources/Evidence.
- **Physical Education**: movement competence, tactics, fitness, safe participation, observation, performance analysis, practice design, and reflection.

Differentiate through access, scaffolding, representation, pace, grouping, and evidence mode without lowering the intended curriculum demand. Build assessment for learning into feedback loops, assessment as learning into self-monitoring, and assessment of learning into valid summative evidence.
